# 🚇 Singapore MRT Facts: the rail network drawn to scale

The official MRT map is a diagram. This page puts every MRT and LRT line and station where it really is on the island, so you can see what the diagram bends.

**👉 [fang89.github.io/Singapore-MRT-facts](https://fang89.github.io/Singapore-MRT-facts/)**

## What's in it

- **True-scale map.** Pan, zoom, search any station, and tap one for its codes, opening year, distance to the next stops and a Google Maps link.
- **Real map ↔ diagram.** Morph between the true geography and a schematic layout generated from the network.
- **Timeline, 1987–2030.** Press play to watch the network grow from Yio Chu Kang–Toa Payoh to the planned Jurong Region and Cross Island Lines.
- **Where the map bends reality.** Station gaps range from 0.4 km to 4.2 km but are drawn nearly equal, the CBD is enlarged many times over, and the Circle Line is not a circle.
- **Fun facts.** The closest two stations are 220 m apart, nine stations sit within 1 km of City Hall, and the network's centre of gravity is almost exactly Caldecott.
- **Connectivity score.** Pick destinations and weights, and every station gets a 0–100 score from travel time, with a 5-minute penalty per line change and short walking links.

## Running it

It's one self-contained `index.html` with no build step. Open it in a browser or serve the folder.

## Data

- Line and station positions: [sgraildata](https://github.com/cheeaun/sgraildata) (OpenStreetMap-derived, © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL)
- Coastline and planning areas: public URA boundaries
- Future station positions: URA Master Plan 2019 rail station layer
- Opening dates: LTA line stages as listed on Wikipedia. Planned dates are LTA's latest targets and may change.

Distances are straight-line between platforms. This is an independent map, not an LTA or SMRT product.
